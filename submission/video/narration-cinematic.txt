Most days, nothing happens.

Mona makes tea. Finishes work. Comes home late. Lives her life without a camera watching, a microphone listening, or a map following every step.

But sometimes a small question remains open: did she get home? Did the evening check-in happen? Is someone already looking into it?

In Case Of begins with one quiet promise: if an expected moment passes unresolved, someone notices.

Mona describes the plan in ordinary language. A nightly check-in. A journey home. A solo hike. Recovery after treatment. The agent turns that sentence into a literal draft: when the moment is due, how long to wait, and who may be contacted, in order.

Then the software slows everything down. Mona sees every step before anything can begin. Saving creates a draft. Monitoring starts only after the required people consent, the channels are ready, and Mona explicitly activates it.

The model interprets language. It never owns the timer, the contact list, or the safety state.

For this judge demo, time is compressed, but the deployed path is real. EventBridge Scheduler opens the due Moment. Step Functions advances the approved ladder. DynamoDB stores the plan, the Alert, and its audit trail. SQS carries durable delivery intents to the only worker allowed to contact a provider.

The screen follows those events as they happen: check requested, self-contact, reminder, escalation. For judges, the final message goes to a clearly labelled safe sink, so no real person is disturbed.

When the uncertainty remains, Maya receives one expiring link for this Alert. She needs no account and no app install.

“I’m checking” creates a short lease. It tells everyone that a human is acting, but it does not close the Alert. If Maya disappears, escalation can continue. Only an explicit outcome—“I reached Mona; she is okay”—resolves the check.

Acknowledged is never mistaken for resolved.

That distinction shapes the entire system. Every Alert is pinned to the exact plan Mona approved. Every action has a durable identity. Concurrent or replayed queue messages cannot repeat a confirmed provider call. Consent and recipient membership are checked again immediately before delivery. If a provider may have accepted a message before timing out, the result enters reconciliation. The system does not blindly resend, and it does not invent success.

The agent is governed structurally. Its tools accept abstract roles, never raw phone numbers, emails, or arbitrary URLs. Cedar begins from default deny. The target repeats tenant, consent, membership, channel, plan-version, Alert-state, and lease checks before any side effect.

The same synthetic flow runs on the public web and in the signed Android release. The APK shown here came from a protected GitHub workflow and ran this deployed Drill across Scheduler, workflow, queue, worker, responder lease, and explicit resolution.

AWS account verification currently blocks the live Nova and AgentCore invocation, so the demo says that plainly and uses a validated deterministic template. There is no fabricated model trace. The safety workflow continues because it was never entrusted to the model.

In Case Of asks for no continuous location, camera, or microphone access. Most days, it stays quiet. On the unusual day, it gives the right people a calm, auditable way to close uncertainty.

In Case Of. Someone notices.
